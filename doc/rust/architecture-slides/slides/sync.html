Synchronous means the caller writes read(key) and gets the row back, with no callbacks. Form A exists today: the application thread is itself a user thread with a connection, and flush or wait_for waits for its reply. It suits tools, tests and low-rate services. Form B is for servers with a thread per request: the thread hands the call to an executor, the same one as on the previous slides, and sleeps until the executor wakes it with the answer. Under load the executor sends many callers' calls together, so the cost per call approaches the pipelined figure. The price is two hand-offs; with the Mac calibration a wake-up costs about 8 microseconds of latency and 3 of CPU.
